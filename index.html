How the calculations work

A percentage of a number

Divide the percentage by 100, then multiply by the number. For example, 15% of 200 is 30. A 15% discount on $200 saves $30.

One number as a percentage of another

Divide the first number by the total, then multiply by 100. For example, 25 out of 200 is 12.5%. The total cannot be zero.

Percentage increase or decrease

Subtract the original value from the new value, divide by the original value, then multiply by 100. Going from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase; going from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease. The original value cannot be zero. Negative original values can produce results that need extra interpretation.

Results are rounded for display to a maximum of six decimal places.

Worked percentage examples

Find a sale price

A $200 item is 15% off. Choose “What is X% of Y?” and enter 15 and 200. The result, 30, is the discount, not the final price. Subtract it from $200: $200 − $30 = $170, before any applicable taxes or fees.

Work out a score

You answered 36 of 40 questions correctly. Choose “X is what percent of Y?” and enter 36 and 40. The calculation is 36 ÷ 40 × 100 = 90%. The second number is the total possible score.

Compare an old price with a new price

A price rises from $80 to $100. Choose “Percentage change from X to Y,” with 80 as the original value and 100 as the new value. The $20 increase is divided by the original $80, giving 25%. Reversing the numbers gives −20%, because the starting value is now $100.

Compare percentage rates

A rate moving from 10% to 15% rises by 5 percentage points. Its relative percentage increase is 50%, calculated as (15 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100. Percentage points measure the difference between rates; percentage change measures the difference relative to the starting rate.

Common questions

Which calculation should I choose?

Use the first mode when you know a percentage and want an amount. Use the second when you know a part and a total and want a percentage. Use the third when you want to compare an original value with a new value. Keep both amounts in the same units.

What happens when the starting value is zero?

Percentage change from zero is undefined because its formula divides by the starting value. You can still describe the absolute change: from 0 to 20 is an increase of 20 units. Similarly, a part cannot be expressed as a percentage of a zero total.

Can I enter negative values or percentages above 100?

Yes. For example, 150% of 20 is 30. Negative values follow the displayed formulas, but percentage change with a negative starting value can be counterintuitive. A change from −10 to −5 gives −50% mathematically, even though the value increased. Consider reporting the absolute change as well.

How precise are the results?

Results use browser floating-point arithmetic and are rounded to at most six decimal places for display. A very small nonzero result may display as zero. Use appropriate precision and independently check figures before relying on them for important decisions.

Do I need an account to calculate?

No account or download is needed. The calculator runs directly in your browser.
